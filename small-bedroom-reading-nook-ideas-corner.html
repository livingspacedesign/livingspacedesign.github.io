Small Bedroom Reading Nook Ideas in a Corner

Intro

A reading nook does not need a whole room — one corner of a small bedroom is enough. One chair, one lamp, one shelf, and suddenly the bedroom has a place that is not for sleeping or working, but for sitting down with a book. It is the cheapest kind of luxury: most of these ideas cost $10–$40, and the corner is already there, doing nothing.

This guide has 12 reading nook ideas for a bedroom corner. Each one fits in about one square metre, and none of them needs a big piece of furniture or a permanent change.

1. One good chair, angled into the corner — $20–$40

A small armchair, a wide dining chair with a cushion, or even a bean bag, angled at 45 degrees into the corner, is the whole nook. The angle matters — it makes the corner feel like a place, not a gap. Look in second-hand shops: a solid wooden chair with a cushion costs less than a new one and lasts longer.

2. A floor cushion and a folded throw — $10–$20

If there is no room for a chair, a large floor cushion and a folded throw do the same job. Sit on the cushion, lean against the wall, and the throw keeps you warm. It folds away in seconds, so the corner is free again in the morning. Choose a cushion with a washable cover — it will get used every day.

3. A clip-on reading lamp — $8–$12

A lamp that clips onto a shelf, the headboard or a small table gives light exactly where the book is, without lighting the whole room. A warm bulb (2700K) is relaxing, and a flexible neck means you can point it at the page. Clip-on means no floor space and no nightstand needed.

4. A floating shelf for books — $10–$15

One small floating shelf at sitting height holds the three books you are reading now, plus a cup of tea. It takes no floor space, and if you rent, adhesive brackets rated for 5 kg will hold it. Keep only the current books on it — the rest stay on the main shelf, or the nook becomes a second bookcase.

5. A small side table, or a stool — $10–$20

A small wooden stool, 30 cm across, holds a cup and a phone beside the chair. It doubles as a seat when a friend visits, and it is lighter and narrower than a table, so it fits in gaps a table cannot. Put a tray on top so small things do not fall off.

6. A throw and a cushion in one colour — $12–$18

One throw and one cushion, in a colour that matches the bedding, make the corner look deliberate instead of accidental. The throw goes over your knees in winter, and the cushion supports your back. Two items, one colour, and the corner stops looking like leftover space.

7. A plant in the corner — $8–$12

One tall plant in the corner, or a small one on the shelf, makes the nook feel alive. Green is calming, and plants clean the air too. A snake plant or a pothos needs almost no care and survives low light. One plant is enough — two is a jungle, and a jungle is not a reading nook.

8. A curtain to divide the corner — $12–$20

Hang a curtain rod across the corner and a single curtain panel, and the nook becomes a small room inside the room. The curtain softens the corner, hides any marks on the wall, and gives you privacy when someone else is in the bedroom. Choose a fabric that matches the window curtains so the room looks planned.

9. A basket for books and magazines — $8–$12

A woven basket beside the chair holds the books, magazines and notebooks you are reading now. It keeps them off the floor and off the bed, and it looks like decor, not storage. One basket, not three — the nook is for reading, not for storing every book you own.

10. A window corner, if you have one — $0–$10

If the corner has a window, the nook is already half built. Put the chair by the window, add a cushion, and the daylight does the rest. A deep windowsill holds a cup and a book, so you do not even need a table. Morning light is the best reading light there is, and it costs nothing.

11. Warm fairy lights along the shelf — $5–$8

A string of warm fairy lights draped along the shelf or the top of the wall makes the corner feel magical at night. They cost about $6, use almost no electricity, and turn an ordinary corner into a place you want to sit. Battery ones need no socket; plug-in ones are brighter.

12. One piece of wall art above the chair — $5–$15

One framed print or a small hanging above the chair gives the nook a focal point and makes it feel finished. Use adhesive picture strips so there are no holes in the wall. Choose something calm — a landscape, a quote, a simple shape — and keep the frame light so it is easy to move.

Conclusion

A reading nook is not about furniture — it is about claiming one corner and giving it a job. A chair, a lamp, a shelf and a throw cost about $50 together, and they turn a dead corner into the best seat in the flat. Start with the chair, add the lamp, and keep only the books you are reading now within reach.

Budget recap

Small chair or floor cushion: ~$25

Clip-on reading lamp: ~$10

Floating shelf: ~$12

Throw + cushion: ~$15

**Total for a full reading nook: ~$62 — one corner, one square metre, one good habit.**
