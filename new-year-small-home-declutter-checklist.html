Small Home

New Year Small Home Declutter Checklist

Intro

January is the best month to declutter a small home, because the Christmas decorations are coming down anyway and every cupboard is already half open. This checklist goes room by room, one small step at a time. Do one step a day, and the whole flat is done in two weeks — no big weekend, no mess in the middle of the room.

1. Start with the front door — 10 minutes, $0

Clear the hooks, the shoe rack, and the basket by the door. One coat per person on the hooks, two pairs of shoes each by the door, and everything else goes to the wardrobe or out of the flat. The entrance sets the tone for the whole home.

2. The junk drawer — 15 minutes, $0

Empty one drawer completely. Throw away dead batteries, dried pens, and old cables. Keep only what you used in the last month. Put small things in one box or a cut-up cereal box as a divider.

3. The living room: one basket, one rule — 15 minutes, $0

Walk the living room with a basket and take out everything that does not belong there. Then apply the rule: if it has no place in this room, it leaves the room. Magazines older than a month go in the recycling.

4. Books and magazines — 20 minutes, $0

Keep the books you will read again or that you love to look at. The rest go in a bag for donation. A small shelf with 10 books you love looks better than a shelf full of books you never open.

5. The wardrobe: the hanger trick — 30 minutes, $0

Turn all hangers around. Every time you wear something, hang it back the right way. In a month, the hangers still turned around hold the clothes you never wear — those go out. It is the easiest wardrobe clear-out there is.

6. Under the bed — 20 minutes, $0

Pull out every box and bag from under the bed. If you have not opened it in a year, open it now: keep, donate, or throw away. Vacuum the floor before anything goes back, and only the things you actually use go back.

7. The kitchen: one shelf at a time — 20 minutes, $0

Do one shelf, not the whole kitchen. Throw away expired food, chipped mugs, and lids with no container. Keep only the mugs, plates, and pans you use every week. A small kitchen works when everything in it is used.

8. The bathroom cabinet — 15 minutes, $0

Empty the cabinet and the drawer. Throw away anything expired, empty, or unused for a year. Keep one of each thing you use daily, and put the rest in one box. A clear bathroom shelf makes the whole morning faster.

9. Cables and chargers — 10 minutes, $0

Gather every cable and charger in the flat into one pile. Keep one of each type you use, label it, and recycle or donate the rest. Old phone chargers are the most common clutter in a small home.

10. The sentimental box — 20 minutes, $0

Choose one box — a shoebox is enough — for the things you keep for memories. Photos, letters, and small keepsakes go in it. When the box is full, something must leave before anything new goes in. One box keeps memories safe without filling the flat.

11. Paper: scan it or shred it — 20 minutes, $0

Old bills, manuals, and letters: scan what you need with your phone, then shred or recycle the paper. Keep only passports, contracts, and certificates in one folder. Paper takes up more space than people think.

12. The "maybe" box — 5 minutes to set up, $0

For the things you cannot decide on, put them in one box with today's date on it. If you have not opened the box in three months, donate it unopened. The maybe box is how you declutter without the guilt.

13. Donate the bags the same day — $0

Put every donation bag by the front door and take it out the same day, or at the latest the next morning. Bags that sit in the flat for a week always get opened again, and the clutter comes back.

14. The 10-minute nightly reset — $0, every evening

Every evening, 10 minutes: one basket, every room, everything back in its place. A decluttered home only stays decluttered if it is maintained, and 10 minutes a night is all it takes.

Conclusion — Done is better than perfect

If you only do three things, do these: clear the front door, do the wardrobe hanger trick, and take the donation bags out the same day. The flat will already feel different, and the rest of the checklist will feel easy.

Budget recap: This whole checklist costs $0. If you want to help it along: 3 storage boxes $10–$15, labels $2–$3, one donation run $0. Total: $0–$18, and the only real cost is about 20 minutes a day for two weeks.
