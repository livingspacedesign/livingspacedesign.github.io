Small Bathroom Mirror Ideas That Double the Light

Intro

A mirror is the cheapest way to make a small bathroom feel bigger. It doubles the light, doubles the sense of space, and costs far less than new tiles or a new vanity. A bare wall above the sink wastes the one surface that can do the most work.

This guide has 12 mirror ideas for small bathrooms. Each one lists what it costs, and most of them need no drilling — adhesive strips and over-door hooks do the job. All are renter-friendly.

1. One large mirror over the sink — $20–$35

The simplest fix of all: replace a small mirror with the largest one that fits above the sink. A mirror that spans the width of the vanity reflects the whole room, not just your face. Look for a plain frameless mirror — it costs less and looks bigger than a framed one the same size.

2. A round mirror to soften hard lines — $15–$25

A round mirror softens all the straight lines in a bathroom — tiles, sink, cabinet. Hang it centred over the sink, about 15 cm above the tap. Round mirrors suit small bathrooms because they have no corners to crowd the wall.

3. An arched mirror for a taller feel — $25–$40

An arched mirror draws the eye upward, so a low ceiling feels higher. The curve adds style without any colour or clutter. Choose an arch as tall as the space allows — height is what makes this one work.

4. A mirrored cabinet that hides clutter — $25–$45

A shallow mirrored cabinet holds medicines, toothpaste and contact lens supplies behind the mirror. Choose one no deeper than 15 cm, or it will crowd the room. A cabinet does two jobs at once, and the mirror still reflects the light.

5. Two small mirrors side by side — $15–$25

Two matching mirrors over a double-width sink, or one over the sink and one on the side wall, bounce light between them. Two small mirrors cost less than one large one, and the pair looks planned rather than patched.

6. A mirror on the wall opposite the window — $20–$30

Hang a mirror directly opposite the window or the light, so it catches daylight and throws it back into the room. This one placement doubles the light more than any other. Even a plain mirror works — the position does the magic.

7. A backlit LED mirror — $30–$45

A mirror with a warm LED strip around its edge glows softly at night and doubles as a reading light. The strip costs about $8 if you add it to a plain mirror yourself, or buy a ready-made one. Keep the light warm white, not cool white.

8. A full-length mirror on the door — $15–$25

An over-door full-length mirror uses the back of the bathroom door — space that was doing nothing. It lets you check an outfit without leaving the bathroom, and it reflects the whole room when the door is closed. Hang it with over-door hooks, no drilling needed.

9. Mirror tiles as a splashback — $10–$18

Small mirror tiles, stuck in a row as a splashback behind the sink, reflect the light at worktop height. They are cheaper than a big mirror and easy to fit with adhesive. Keep the row low, so splashes wipe off easily.

10. A framed mirror that matches the taps — $15–$30

A thin frame in the same finish as your taps — chrome, brass or black — ties the whole bathroom together. The frame costs nothing extra if you paint a plain wooden frame yourself. Match the finish, and the room looks designed, not assembled.

11. A mirror with a shelf built in — $20–$30

A mirror with a small shelf along its bottom edge holds a toothbrush, a glass and a small plant. It saves the space a separate shelf would take, and everything you need is at hand height. Choose one with a shelf deep enough for a glass, about 10 cm.

12. An old mirror, cleaned and re-hung — $0

The cheapest mirror idea costs nothing. Take the mirror you already have, clean it properly, and hang it where it catches the light — opposite the window, not beside it. A clean mirror reflects twice as much light as a steamed, spotted one.

Conclusion

A mirror is the fastest way to change a small bathroom. One large mirror, placed opposite the light, can double the brightness and make the room feel twice as big — for under $35, without drilling a single hole. Start with the biggest mirror that fits, keep it clean, and let the light do the work.

Budget recap

- Large frameless mirror over the sink: ~$28 - Round or arched mirror: ~$20 - Over-door full-length mirror: ~$18

Total for a brighter bathroom: ~$40 — or ~$28 if you start with one large mirror.
