Small Bathroom Makeover Under $100: One Weekend

Intro

A small bathroom does not need a plumber or a new suite to look new — it needs a deep clean, a declutter, and a few cheap swaps. One weekend, about $89, and the bathroom will feel like a different room.

This guide is a 2-day makeover plan with 12 steps. Each step lists what it costs, and none of them needs a tradesperson — paint, new hardware and soft touches do the job. All are renter-friendly. The plan below splits the work across two days — Saturday for the messy jobs, Sunday for the pretty ones.

1. Saturday morning: deep clean everything — $5–$8

Start with a deep clean — tiles, grout, taps, mirror, floor. A grout pen and a bottle of limescale remover cost about $7 and make the bathroom look five years younger. Clean first, so you can see what actually needs changing.

2. Declutter the vanity and cabinet — $0

Take everything out of the vanity and the cabinet. Throw away anything empty, expired or unused in the past year. What is left goes back in groups — daily items on a tray, spares in a basket under the sink. A clear vanity is half the makeover.

3. Paint one wall — $12–$18

Paint one wall pale sage or blush pink, and leave the rest white. One small tin of paint is enough for one wall, and it comes off with one coat of white when you move out. Use a small roller for a smooth finish, and tape the edges before you start. Paint on Saturday, so it dries overnight.

4. Swap the shower curtain — $10–$15

A new shower curtain in a plain colour — navy, sage or terracotta — changes the whole room for about $12. Choose a plain curtain, not a patterned one. Hang it on Saturday, so the room feels new by Sunday.

5. New towels in one colour — $12–$18

Two new towels in one accent colour tie the whole bathroom together. Match the towels to the painted wall, and the room will look planned. Wash the towels first, so they are soft and ready.

6. Saturday afternoon: new hardware — $10–$15

Swap the cabinet handles, the towel rail or the toilet roll holder for new ones in one finish — chrome, brass or black. New hardware costs a few dollars per piece and makes old furniture look new. Keep all the hardware in the same finish. Keep the old handles in a bag, so you can put them back when you move out.

7. A new mirror or a bigger one — $20–$30

Replace a small mirror with the largest one that fits above the sink. A bigger mirror doubles the light and makes the room feel twice as big. Hang it opposite the window, so it catches the daylight.

8. Add one plant — $5–$8

A small plant in a pot on the windowsill or on a shelf makes a bathroom feel alive. Plants like humidity, so a bathroom suits them. Choose a plant that likes low light — a pothos or a fern. Water the plant when you clean the bathroom, so you never forget.

9. Sunday morning: a tray and matching bottles — $8–$12

A small tray on the vanity holds hand cream, a small mirror and a candle in one tidy group. Two matching refillable bottles replace mismatched shop bottles and make the vanity look planned. Everything in one place, nothing scattered.

10. A wooden bath mat — $10–$15

A wooden bath mat feels warm underfoot, dries fast, and looks like a spa. It costs about $12 and replaces a damp fabric mat that never dries. Choose a slatted mat, so water drains through it.

11. Sunday afternoon: soft touches — $5–$8

A scented candle in a jar, a small framed print, and a hanging bunch of dried eucalyptus. Three soft touches cost about $7 and make the bathroom smell and feel like a spa. Keep the print small, so it does not crowd the wall.

12. Sunday evening: the 10-minute reset — $0

The last step costs nothing. Put everything back in its place, wipe the mirror, and take one photo of the finished room. Then agree on a 10-minute weekly reset — every Sunday, just 10 minutes, and the makeover lasts. Keep the photo on your phone, so you can see what the room should look like.

Conclusion

A small bathroom makeover does not need a new suite — it needs a clean, a painted wall, and a few swaps. Paint, a new curtain, new towels and new hardware cost about $89, take one weekend, and make the bathroom feel brand new. Start on Saturday morning with a deep clean, and by Sunday evening the room will feel like a spa.

Budget recap

- Paint + grout pen + cleaning supplies: ~$20 - Shower curtain + towels: ~$25 - New hardware + mirror: ~$35 - Plant + tray + candle: ~$15

Total for the weekend makeover: ~$89 — or ~$20 if you start with a clean and a painted wall.
