Small Bathroom Storage

Small Bathroom Storage Ideas Over the Toilet

Intro

The wall above the toilet is the most wasted space in a small bathroom. It is empty, it is at hand height, and it is big enough for a whole shelf unit — yet most flats leave it bare. The fix is simple: build up, not out.

This guide has 12 over-toilet storage ideas. Each one lists what it costs, and most of them need no drilling — adhesive hooks, leaning units and freestanding shelves do the job. All are renter-friendly.

1. A freestanding over-toilet shelf unit — $25–$40

A tall shelf unit that stands over the toilet, with its legs on either side, gives three shelves of storage without a single hole in the wall. Towels, toilet paper and baskets all fit. Measure the width of your toilet first — most units fit a standard 50 cm bowl.

2. Two floating shelves above the cistern — $12–$20

Two short floating shelves, fixed with adhesive strips or small screws, hold baskets and toiletries above the cistern. Keep the lower shelf above head height when you stand up, so nothing bumps you. Two shelves cost about $15.

3. A small cabinet over the toilet — $20–$35

A shallow wall cabinet with a mirrored door hides medicines and toiletries, and the mirror makes the bathroom feel bigger. Choose one no deeper than 15 cm, or it will crowd the room. A mirrored cabinet does two jobs at once.

4. Baskets on one shelf — $8–$12

One shelf plus three matching baskets is the cheapest over-toilet storage there is. One basket for toilet paper, one for cleaning supplies, one for spare towels. Matching baskets look tidy, and you can see what is inside without opening anything.

5. A ladder shelf leaning on the wall — $20–$30

A leaning ladder shelf stands against the wall above the toilet, with wide shelves at the bottom and narrow ones at the top. It needs no fixing at all — its own weight holds it. It suits bathrooms with a little floor space beside the toilet.

6. A towel rail above the toilet — $8–$12

A towel rail fixed high on the wall above the toilet holds two rolled towels, always within reach. It costs a few dollars, takes ten minutes to fit, and uses space that was completely empty. Roll the towels so they look neat.

7. A plant shelf for a bit of green — $5–$10

One small shelf with a trailing plant above the toilet makes a small bathroom feel alive. Plants like humidity, so a bathroom suits them. Choose a plant that likes low light, and water it when you clean the bathroom.

8. A tray for the things you use daily — $5–$8

A small tray on the shelf holds the items you reach for every day — hand cream, a small mirror, a scented candle. Everything in one place, nothing scattered. A tray with raised edges stops things sliding off.

9. Hooks on the side of the shelf unit — $3–$6

Stick-on hooks on the side of an over-toilet unit hold a hand towel, a bathrobe, or a small bag. The sides of the unit were empty — now they work too. Use hooks rated for 1 kg so nothing falls.

10. Storage boxes with labels — $6–$10

Two or three labelled boxes on the top shelf hold the things you use least — spare soap, first-aid items, cleaning cloths. Labels mean you find them without opening every box. Clear boxes work best, so you can see inside.

11. A mirror cabinet instead of a plain mirror — $18–$30

If your bathroom mirror is plain, swap it for a shallow mirror cabinet above the toilet. It stores a surprising amount — medicines, toothpaste, contact lens supplies — and the mirror still does its job. It is the best use of that wall.

12. Keep the top shelf for spares only — $0

The top shelf is for spares: extra toilet paper, a spare towel, the next bottle of soap. Nothing you need daily goes up there, because reaching it is a stretch. A shelf that holds only spares stays tidy, and the daily items stay at hand height.

Conclusion

The space above the toilet is a whole storage unit waiting to happen. A freestanding shelf, two floating shelves, or a small cabinet can hold everything a small bathroom needs, for under $40, without drilling a single hole. Start with one shelf and three baskets, and the bathroom will feel twice as big.

Budget recap

- Freestanding over-toilet unit: ~$32

- Two floating shelves: ~$15

- Baskets + hooks + labels: ~$12

Total for a complete over-toilet setup: ~$40 — or ~$15 if you start with shelves and baskets.
