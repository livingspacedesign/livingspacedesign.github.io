Homemade Christmas Decorations — 12 Easy DIY Ideas

Intro

Homemade Christmas decorations cost almost nothing, look better than shop-bought ones, and make the flat feel personal. A paper star you folded yourself, a dried orange slice from your own oven, a salt-dough ornament with a child's fingerprint — none of these can be bought, and all of them mean more than a bauble from a box. In a small flat, homemade decorations are also the practical choice: they are light, they store flat, and they cost pennies.

This guide has 12 homemade Christmas decoration ideas, all easy enough for one afternoon. Each idea lists what it costs. Most of them use things already in the kitchen or the craft drawer. Start with the dried oranges, then the paper stars, and let the flat fill up one handmade piece at a time.

1. Dried orange slices with cinnamon sticks — $3–$5

Dried orange slices are the classic homemade decoration. Slice two oranges as thin as you can, lay them on a baking tray, and dry them in a low oven — about 100°C — for two hours, turning once. Thread them onto string with cinnamon sticks between each slice, and hang the garland on the tree, the mantel or the window. The oranges cost about $3 and the cinnamon about $2. They smell faintly of Christmas for weeks and look warm against green pine.

2. Folded paper stars from parcel paper — $2–$5

A folded paper star looks far more expensive than it is. Cut brown parcel paper into strips, fold them into five-pointed stars — there are free templates online — and hang them from the ceiling on thread, or on the tree. A roll of parcel paper costs about $3 and makes twenty stars. The brown paper looks rustic and warm, and the stars are light enough to hang anywhere. Make them in two sizes: large ones for the window, small ones for the tree.

3. Salt-dough ornaments with fingerprints — $1–$3

Salt dough is flour, salt and water — the cheapest decoration material there is. Mix two cups of flour, one cup of salt and one cup of water, roll it out, cut shapes with biscuit cutters and bake at a low heat until hard. Press a child's fingerprint or a thumbprint into each one before baking, then paint them white and gold. The ingredients cost about $2. Salt-dough ornaments last for years if they are kept dry, and they are the decorations that get kept long after the shop-bought ones are thrown away.

4. A pom-pom garland from leftover wool — $0–$5

If you have leftover wool, you have a garland. Wind wool around a fork or a piece of card, tie it in the middle, cut the loops and fluff it into a pom-pom. Make ten or twelve in different colours, thread them onto string, and drape the garland across the mantel. Leftover wool costs nothing; a ball of wool costs about $3. Pom-poms are soft, light and impossible to break, which makes them the safest homemade decoration in a small flat.

5. Popcorn and cranberry garland — $3–$6

Popcorn and cranberries threaded onto string make the oldest homemade garland, and it still works. Pop a bag of plain popcorn, thread the pieces onto a long needle with cranberries between them, and drape the garland around the tree. The popcorn costs about $2 and the cranberries about $3. It looks charming on a small tree, smells faintly of the kitchen, and the birds eat it in January if you hang it outside. Keep it away from damp rooms — popcorn goes soft in humidity.

6. Cinnamon stick ornaments tied with twine — $3–$5

Bundle three cinnamon sticks together with twine, tie a small bow on top, and hang the bundle on the tree. It takes two minutes, costs about $4 for a bag of cinnamon sticks and a ball of twine, and smells wonderful every time you walk past the tree. Make six or eight bundles and space them evenly around the tree. Cinnamon bundles also work as napkin holders on the Christmas table — one per place setting, tied with a sprig of rosemary.

7. Pinecone ornaments dipped in white paint — $0–$4

Pinecones are free from any park. Dip the tips in white paint, or brush them lightly with glue and sprinkle on glitter, and they look like snow-dusted trees. A small pot of white paint costs about $3. Tie a loop of twine to the top of each pinecone and hang them on the tree, or pile them in a bowl on the table as a centrepiece. Pinecones are the easiest homemade decoration: find them, paint them, hang them.

8. A paper snowflake window display — $0–$2

Fold white paper into snowflakes — the kind you made at school — and stick them to the window with a little water. They cost nothing, they look beautiful from the street, and they come off the glass in seconds. Make them in three sizes and stick them in a loose cluster on the largest window, not in a neat row. Add one string of fairy lights around the window frame, and the snowflakes glow from inside. This is the homemade decoration that costs the least and gets the most compliments.

9. A twig star for the top of the tree — $0–$3

Instead of a shop-bought star, make one from twigs. Gather five straight twigs from the park, tie them into a star shape with twine, and wrap the star in fairy lights or gold thread. The twigs are free and the twine costs about $2. A twig star looks rustic and handmade, it is light enough for a small tree, and it is the one decoration on the tree that nobody else will have. It also packs flat in January.

10. Handmade gift tags from old cards — $0–$2

Cut last year's Christmas cards into gift tags. The pictures on old cards — robins, trees, snowmen — make perfect tags, and cutting them up costs nothing. Punch a hole in each tag, thread it with string, and write the name in pen. A hole punch costs about $2 if you do not own one. Handmade gift tags make even a plain brown-paper parcel look thoughtful, and they use up cards that would otherwise go in the bin.

11. A felt garland of trees and stars — $4–$8

Cut simple shapes — trees, stars, stockings — from coloured felt, and sew or glue them onto a length of ribbon. A few sheets of felt cost about $5 and make a garland 2 metres long. Felt is soft, it does not fray, and the shapes are easy enough for a child to cut. Hang the garland across the mantel or along the top of a bookcase. A felt garland lasts for years, folds flat for storage, and looks better every year as the colours fade a little.

12. A simmer pot on the stove as decoration — $2–$4

A simmer pot is not a decoration you hang — it is one you smell. Put orange slices, cinnamon sticks, cloves and a few cranberries in a pot of water on the stove, and let it simmer gently. The whole flat smells of Christmas within ten minutes. The ingredients cost about $3. Make a simmer pot on Christmas morning, and again on New Year's Eve, and the flat smells like the holidays without a single candle or spray. It is the homemade decoration that works on a sense no other decoration can reach.

Conclusion

Homemade Christmas decorations are the cheapest, lightest and most personal decorations you can own. Dried oranges, paper stars, salt dough, pinecones and pom-poms cost almost nothing, store flat, and make a small flat feel like it belongs to the people who live there. Start with one afternoon, one oven and one pair of scissors — and by the end of December, half the flat will be handmade.

Budget recap

- Dried oranges + cinnamon: ~$5 - Paper stars + snowflakes: ~$3 - Salt dough + pinecones: ~$5

Total for homemade decorations: ~$10–$20 — start with the oranges, then the paper stars.
