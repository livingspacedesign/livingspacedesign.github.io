Small Kitchen

Small Kitchen Organization for January

Intro

January is the month the kitchen gets honest. Christmas leftovers are gone, the cupboards are full of things you forgot you owned, and there is never enough counter space. In a small kitchen, organisation is not about buying more storage — it is about using the space you already have, one shelf at a time.

1. Empty one cupboard, not the whole kitchen — $0

Start with the cupboard you open most. Take everything out, wipe the shelf, throw away anything expired, and put back only what you use every week. One cupboard done properly beats a whole kitchen half done.

2. Group by use, not by type — $0

Put the coffee things together, the baking things together, and the everyday plates together. When everything for one job is in one place, you stop opening three cupboards to make one cup of tea.

3. A shelf riser doubles one shelf — $8–$12

A small metal shelf riser inside a cupboard turns one shelf into two. Put plates below and bowls above, or mugs below and small jars above. It is the cheapest way to double cupboard space.

4. Hooks on the inside of the door — $5–$8

Stick adhesive hooks on the inside of a cupboard door for measuring spoons, a small towel, or a bin bag roll. The inside of the door is free space in every small kitchen, and it costs nothing to use.

5. One drawer for cooking tools — $0–$5

Put all spatulas, ladles, and tongs in one drawer, and everything else somewhere else. If a tool does not fit in that drawer, it does not belong in the kitchen. A drawer divider made from a cut-up box works fine.

6. Jars you can see through — $10–$15

Move rice, pasta, flour, and sugar into clear glass jars, all the same size. You can see what is running low, the shelf looks tidy, and square jars waste less space than round ones. Keep the labels on the lids.

7. The wall is a shelf — $10–$15

A hanging rail with 4–5 hooks on the wall holds utensils, mugs, or scissors. It frees a whole drawer, and everything is within reach while you cook. Use adhesive hooks if you rent and cannot drill.

8. The top of the fridge — $0–$5

Put a basket on top of the fridge for the things you use least — the picnic set, the big roasting tray, the spare foil. It is dead space in most kitchens, and one basket makes it useful.

9. A tray for oils and sauces — $3–$5

Put cooking oil, vinegar, and soy sauce on one small tray beside the stove. They stay together, the shelf stays clean, and you can lift the whole tray out to wipe underneath. One tray stops the sticky-bottle problem.

10. The under-sink zone — $5–$8

Put cleaning supplies in one small caddy under the sink, and hang a bag holder on the door. Everything else under the sink goes out. The under-sink cupboard is small, so it only holds cleaning things — nothing else.

11. A weekly fridge check — $0

Every Sunday, five minutes: throw away anything past its date, move tonight's dinner to the front, and wipe one shelf. A small fridge only works if it is checked weekly, because there is no room for forgotten food.

12. One chopping board, one good knife — $0

Keep one large board and one sharp knife out, and store the rest. Two boards and five knives in a small kitchen just take up counter space. A sharp knife is safer and faster than five blunt ones.

13. Labels on everything — $2–$3

Label the jars, the boxes, and the shelves. Labels stop the "where is it?" problem, and they stop other people putting things in the wrong place. A roll of masking tape and a pen is all you need.

14. The clear counter rule — $0

At the end of every day, clear the counter completely. Put away the drying rack, the chopping board, and the kettle goes back to its corner. A clear counter in the morning makes the whole kitchen feel bigger, and it takes two minutes at night.

Conclusion — Start with the cupboard you open most

If you only do three things, do these: empty the cupboard you open most, group things by use, and add one shelf riser. The kitchen will feel organised by tonight, and the rest can follow one shelf at a time.

Budget recap: Shelf riser $8–$12, hanging rail $10–$15, clear jars $10–$15, hooks and tray $8–$13, labels $2–$3. Total for the full organisation: about $38–$58, and the free habits — the weekly fridge check and the clear counter rule — cost $0.
